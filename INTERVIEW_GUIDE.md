# Business360 — Interview Discussion Guide

This guide helps explain the four portfolio case studies clearly and honestly in an interview. The case studies are **synthetic exercises**. Discuss the decisions and proposed methods as portfolio work; do not describe them as projects delivered for an employer or client.

## A reliable 90-second structure

1. **Context:** What business situation is being modelled?
2. **Decision:** What does the decision-maker need to decide?
3. **Diagnosis:** What evidence would distinguish symptoms from root causes?
4. **Recommendation:** What would you do first, and why?
5. **Trade-off:** What are you deliberately not doing?
6. **Measurement:** What evidence would justify scaling, changing, or stopping?
7. **Limitation:** What remains unvalidated?

A strong answer names the decision and trade-off before listing frameworks.

## OPS-001 — Annotation delivery workflow

**Decision:** How can an annotation operation improve delivery predictability without weakening quality?

- **Diagnosis:** Trace batch flow from intake to acceptance; inspect readiness failures, complexity mix, defect categories, rework ageing, and delivery misses.
- **Recommendation:** Establish intake/readiness gates, complexity-weighted allocation, guideline version control, calibrated QA, owned rework queues, and a batch-level control tracker.
- **Key trade-off:** More disciplined readiness and QA can add front-end effort, but may prevent downstream rework and late delivery surprises.
- **Pilot:** Four weeks on one representative workstream, with a validated baseline and explicit quality guardrails.
- **Scale evidence:** Better delivery/flow outcomes with stable or improved first-pass acceptance, no critical control failure, and repeatability across work types.
- **Do not claim:** That the proposed SOP was implemented, that the illustrative baseline is real, or that any target was achieved.

**Likely questions**
- Why use weighted workload rather than task count?
- How would you distinguish a training issue from an unclear guideline?
- What would make you stop the pilot even if throughput improved?
- How would you prevent QA sampling from hiding rare critical defects?

## MKT-001 — B2B go-to-market

**Decision:** Which buyer segment and workflow problem should a small B2B services firm target first?

- **Diagnosis:** Validate pain frequency/cost, buyer urgency, access, willingness to pay, procurement friction, delivery fit, and alternatives.
- **Recommendation:** Focus on one beachhead segment, run structured discovery, offer a scoped paid pilot, and expand only after commercial and delivery repeatability are demonstrated.
- **Key trade-off:** Narrow focus reduces apparent market reach but improves learning speed, message relevance, and sales discipline.
- **Validation:** Proposed 12–15 discovery interviews, followed by cohort-based funnel tracking and pilot conversion analysis.
- **Scale evidence:** Qualified demand, viable acquisition economics, customer-confirmed value, and delivery capacity.
- **Do not claim:** That interviews, campaigns, customer conversions, or revenue outcomes occurred.

**Likely questions**
- Why not launch across multiple verticals?
- What makes an opportunity qualified?
- Why use a paid pilot rather than a free proof of concept?
- Which funnel metric would you distrust if viewed alone?

## STR-001 — B2B market-entry decision

**Decision:** Should a services company enter a new vertical, and what evidence should precede investment?

- **Diagnosis:** Compare segment attractiveness with capability fit, access, competitive alternatives, economics, procurement friction, and risk.
- **Recommendation:** Use a staged entry process: screen, validate, prove, establish, then expand.
- **Key trade-off:** A beachhead limits near-term breadth but reduces execution dilution and creates a clearer learning loop.
- **Validation:** Buyer research, source-backed secondary research, sensitivity-tested scoring, and a scoped pilot with unit economics.
- **Scale evidence:** Validated pain and buying authority, delivery reliability, credible margin, and repeatability.
- **Do not claim:** That a real market was researched, a segment selected, or investment approved.

**Likely questions**
- How would you avoid false precision in weighted scoring?
- Which criteria should be treated as veto gates rather than weighted factors?
- How would you account for opportunity cost?
- What would cause you to defer entry?

## AI-001 — AI-assisted quality review

**Decision:** Is AI assistance preferable to process or rule-based improvement for a defined quality-review task?

- **Diagnosis:** Establish baseline review effort, defect escape, task repeatability, ground-truth quality, data permissions, and failure cost.
- **Recommendation:** Improve the process first, use deterministic rules where possible, then test AI in shadow mode before any bounded assisted mode.
- **Key trade-off:** Human oversight and evaluation add cost, but reduce the risk of automation bias and uncontrolled quality failures.
- **Validation:** Representative permissioned evaluation data, independent adjudication, precision/recall, class-level analysis, reviewer time, total cost, auditability, and rollback.
- **Scale evidence:** Agreed critical-defect thresholds, no unresolved privacy/security issues, net value after total costs, and workable human override.
- **Do not claim:** That a model was deployed, evaluated, or produced savings.

**Likely questions**
- Why not automate the whole review process?
- Why are precision and recall both necessary?
- What is the difference between released capacity and cash savings?
- How would you handle model drift and reviewer over-reliance?

## How to connect the portfolio to actual experience

When discussing professional experience, use a separate, factual example from your own work. Structure it as:

- **Situation:** The real operating or customer context, without disclosing confidential information.
- **Responsibility:** Your actual remit and decision authority.
- **Action:** What you personally did versus what the wider team did.
- **Evidence:** A verified result, or a qualitative outcome if no reliable metric is available.
- **Learning:** What you would repeat or change.

Be precise about team size: distinguish direct reports, total workforce, and cross-functional stakeholders. Do not imply a reporting relationship that did not exist.

## Final interview discipline

- Say “I designed this synthetic case study” when describing the portfolio exercise.
- Say “I did” only for actions you personally performed in real work.
- Label estimates, targets, and illustrative numbers.
- If a question exposes a missing data point, explain how you would obtain it rather than inventing an answer.
